# Cliffhanger

Notes for working on this game. The repo-wide rules are in the root `CLAUDE.md`, including the shared patterns every back-room game follows; this file lists only what this game adds or does differently. Update this file in the same change as any rule, id or asset change.

## Rules and boundaries

- **Cliffhanger** (`packages/private-games/cliffhanger`, id `cliffhanger`) is a film face-off and describing game for 4 to 16 in two teams (Popcorn 🍿 and Nachos 🌮; newcomers join the smaller one). Never write its film card lists either.
  - **Content packs only:** `films`/`shows` (`{title, genre, tier?}`) and `categories`/`tv-categories` (strings), schemas in `src/logic/content.ts`. The manifest's genre tapes (and the face-off card) are portrait art shown turned onto their side (`SideArt`, boxes mapped by `turnBox`), so every title reads left to right; plain landscape tapes in each genre's colour without it.
  - **No app judging:** players decide out loud whether an answer counts or a describer slipped; nothing pauses a face-off.
  - **Rules:** a face-off is a rally of Said it! presses, each resetting the answer clock; running out loses. The winner draws 6 and keeps 3, the rest go to the loser (No advantage: 5 each). Each square says how to describe (`one-word`, `quote`, `act`, `describe-badly`, `cast`; Voice call's third square and a player on a call get the host's `actAlt` for Act it: Describe it badly, the default, or Name the cast). The winner's team describes first; all three got opens the other side's cards to steal; a turn with nothing left is skipped. After a round, three of a genre may be traded for one of the other team's; the trailing team may pick the next category; sudden death is an option. The host picks the goal's genres (at least 4; the film presets set them) and 1 or 2 of each (`goalFor`; a saved `goal` alone still reads as its preset); off genres leave the deck, and Start needs `titlesNeeded` titles per picked genre.
  - **`view()`:** a card reaches its describer (only them while it is described), its own team after its turn, the stealing describer once stealing opens, and everyone once got or in the trade; never the team still guessing it, watchers or the table screen.
  - **Sound:** effects only, where the host picks (a paired table screen until then): the clock ticks, three hurry loops over a turn's last 10 s, the buzzer, stings; the describer's phone buzzes at 5 s.
  - **Table screen:** the shared poster (`Stage.tsx`): each team's genres, tapes and people on its side, the moment in the middle (the face-off's category card, the turn's squares as slates, the 120 px clock). The tapes lie flat, side by side, never upright (owner).
  - **Testing:** `CLIFFHANGER_TEST_DECK=1` deals titles and categories in the packs' own order.

- **UI:** Tape titles never go under 10 px, and wherever a tape is small (the finale, the end's shelves, the recap, the phone shelves) its title is repeated in 12 px plain type under it; the keep step shows the offered tapes two a row on phones (three wider) with each square's title also in plain type under it, and the face-off's category card stands large above the category (most of a phone's width, about 400 px on desktops), with Said it! beside it on wide screens so it is never below the fold. The finale reserves the winner headline above a grid of flat tapes across the poster body. The TV countdown occupies the clock slot below the headline. Recap tapes sit two across on phones (four on wide screens); the end gives each team's shelf its own tab on phones (both side by side on wide screens) and the finale's tapes stay one row there; the steal offer is one sideways-scrolling row on phones and a five-across grid on wide screens. On phones the countdown and keep steps, and a turn for everyone but the describer, show both teams' races and won tapes under the step. The table screen shows each side's genres as big tiles four across and its players as coloured name chips.

## Assets

**Cliffhanger** (`node scripts/r2-upload.mjs cliffhanger <folder>`, dry-run first): the genre tapes in `tapes/` (portrait, 538×740, with `tapes/thumb/` copies), the face-off card (536×740, + thumb), the board, the sign, the box, the app pictures (`go`, `timesUp`, `faceoffTitle`, `roundTitle`, `pressStart`, `pressReset`, `buzzer`, `background`), the sound clips, and a `manifest.json` of the form `{ "title", "credit": "…" or { "title", "paragraphs", "links" }, "box", "boxReturns", "board", "sign", "genres": { "<film genre>": { "name", "tape", "thumb", "labelBox": [x, y, w, h] } }, "tvGenres": { "<tv genre>": { "name", "tape", "thumb", "labelBox", "genreBox" } }, "faceoff": { "card", "thumb", "textBox" }, "app": { … }, "audio": { "<cue>": { "file", "fallback", "length" } }, "names": { "genres", "tvGenres", "styles" } }` (shape in `packages/private-games/cliffhanger/src/logic/manifest.ts`; boxes are fractions of the picture, and titles are printed turned 90° clockwise in `labelBox`). Cues are `tick`, `hurry`, `hurry2`, `hurry3`, `buzzer`, `press`, `got`, `pass`, `countdown`, `steal`, `genre` and `win`; `fallback` is played where the browser can't play `file`. Every field is optional: without it, tapes are drawn in each genre's colour and the game is silent. The titles and categories are content packs, never in the repository: `ADMIN_PASSWORD=… node scripts/import-content-pack.mjs <url> cliffhanger films <file.json>` with `{ "films": [{ "title", "genre", "tier"? }] }` (genres `action`, `classics`, `drama`, `comedy`, `horror`, `family`, `animation`, `scifi`, then `heart`, `otherworld`, `remakes`, `adult`; `tier` is `easy` or `medium`), `categories` with `{ "categories": ["…"] }`, and the TV deck's `shows` (genres `action`, `classics`, `drama`, `comedy`, `crime`, `reality`, `animation`, `scifi`) and `tv-categories` the same way. The host picks which genres count, and a game starts only when every picked genre has at least 10 titles (20 for two of each) in the chosen packs. Once it is uploaded, set the title with the `cliffhanger` id. The guide's steps show the face-off card, the board and the box when the manifest has them, and the lobby's pitch shows the face-off card.
